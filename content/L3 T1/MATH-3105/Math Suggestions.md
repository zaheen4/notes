## 1__ 
State green theorem given a vector field $F = siny \hat{\mathbf{i}} + x(1+cosy) \hat{\mathbf{j}}$. Evaluate the line integral $\int_{c}^{} f \, \mathrm{d}r$ given by $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$<br><br>
**Statement of Green's Theorem**

Green's Theorem converts a line integral around a closed curve $C$ into a double integral over the region $D$ enclosed by that curve:

$$\oint_{C} (P \, dx + Q \, dy) = \iint_{D} \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) \, dA$$

**Step 1: Identify P and Q**

From the given vector field $\mathbf{F} = \sin y \, \hat{\mathbf{i}} + x(1+\cos y) \, \hat{\mathbf{j}}$:

- $P = \sin y$
    
- $Q = x + x\cos y$
    

**Step 2: Calculate Derivatives**

Find the partial derivatives required for the formula:

- $\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = 1 + \cos y$
    
- $\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \cos y$
    

**Step 3: Simplify the Integrand**

Subtract the results:

$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = (1 + \cos y) - \cos y = 1$$

**Step 4: Solve the Integral**

Now, evaluate the double integral of $1$ over the region $D$:
$$\oint_{C} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_{D} (1) \, dA$$

Integrating $1$ over a region simply gives the **Area** of that region. The region $D$ is a circle defined by $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$, which has a radius of $a$.

$$\text{Area of Circle} = \pi a^2$$

**Final Answer**

$$\pi a^2$$

## 2__  
Green's theorem $\int_{c}^{}(3x^2 + 8y^2) \, \mathrm{d}x + (4y - 6xy)\, \mathrm{d}y$ bounded by $y=\sqrt x$ and $y = x^2$<br><br>
**The Formula**

Green's Theorem converts the line integral into a double integral:

$$\oint_{C} P \, dx + Q \, dy = \iint_{D} \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) \, dA$$

**Step 1: Simplify the Integrand**

Given $P = 3x^2 + 8y^2$ and $Q = 4y - 6xy$:

1. Differentiate $Q$ with respect to $x$:
    
    $$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = -6y$$
2. Differentiate $P$ with respect to $y$:
    
    $$\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 16y$$
3. Subtract:
$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = -6y - 16y = -22y$$

**Step 2: Determine Limits**

The region is bounded by $y = x^2$ (bottom curve) and $y = \sqrt{x}$ (top curve). Find intersection points: $x^2 = \sqrt{x} \Rightarrow x = 0, 1$.

- **x-limits:** $0$ to $1$
    
- **y-limits:** $x^2$ to $\sqrt{x}$
    

**Step 3: Evaluate Inner Integral (**$dy$**)**

$$\int_{x^2}^{\sqrt{x}} -22y \, dy$$

$$= \left[ -11y^2 \right]_{x^2}^{\sqrt{x}}$$

$$= -11(\sqrt{x})^2 - (-11(x^2)^2)$$

$$= -11(x - x^4)$$

**Step 4: Evaluate Outer Integral (**$dx$**)**

$$-11 \int_{0}^{1} (x - x^4) \, dx = -11 \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_{0}^{1}$$

$$= -11 \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5} \right)$$

$$= -11 \left( \frac{5-2}{10} \right)$$

$$= -11 \left( \frac{3}{10} \right)$$

**Final Answer**

$$-\frac{33}{10} \quad \text{or} \quad -3.3$$

## 3__  
Verify Green''s theorem in the plane $\int_{c}^{}(x^2 + xy) \,  \mathrm{d}x + (x^2 - y^2) \, \mathrm{d}y$ where $c$ is square formed by lines $x = \pm 1 ; y = \pm 1$<br><br>
**Green's Theorem Formula**

$$\oint_{C} (P \, dx + Q \, dy) = \iint_{R} \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) \, dA$$

Given: $P = x^2 + xy$ and $Q = x^2 - y^2$. Region $R$: $-1 \le x \le 1$ and $-1 \le y \le 1$.

**1. Right Hand Side (Double Integral)**

$$\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = 2x, \quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = x$$

$$\text{RHS} = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-1}^{1} (2x - x) \, dy \, dx = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-1}^{1} x \, dy \, dx$$

Integrate with respect to $y$:

$$\int_{-1}^{1} x \, dy = x [y]_{-1}^{1} = x(1 - (-1)) = 2x$$

Integrate with respect to $x$:

$$\text{RHS} = \int_{-1}^{1} 2x \, dx = \left[ x^2 \right]_{-1}^{1} = 1^2 - (-1)^2 = 0$$

**2. Left Hand Side (Line Integral)**

The square has 4 segments. Summing the integrals:

- **Segment 1 (**$y=-1, dy=0, x: -1 \to 1$**):**
    
    $$\int_{-1}^{1} (x^2 - x) \, dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2}{3}$$
    
- **Segment 2 (**$x=1, dx=0, y: -1 \to 1$**):**
    
    $$\int_{-1}^{1} (1 - y^2) \, dy = \left[ y - \frac{y^3}{3} \right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{4}{3}$$
    
- **Segment 3 (**$y=1, dy=0, x: 1 \to -1$**):**
    
    $$\int_{1}^{-1} (x^2 + x) \, dx = -\int_{-1}^{1} (x^2 + x) \, dx = -\frac{2}{3}$$
    
- **Segment 4 (**$x=-1, dx=0, y: 1 \to -1$**):**
    
    $$\int_{1}^{-1} (1 - y^2) \, dy = -\int_{-1}^{1} (1 - y^2) \, dy = -\frac{4}{3}$$
    

**Total Sum:**

$$\text{LHS} = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{4}{3} - \frac{2}{3} - \frac{4}{3} = 0$$

**Result**

$$\text{LHS} = 0, \quad \text{RHS} = 0$$

Theorem Verified.

## 4__ 
[*important*] Find $\int \int_{s}^{} fn \, \mathrm{d}s$ where $f = (2x + 3z)\hat{\mathbf{i}} - (xz+y)\hat{\mathbf{j}} + (y^2 +2z) \hat{\mathbf{k}}$ where $s$ is surface of the sphere having center (3, -1, 2) and radius 3<br><br>
**Divergence Theorem**

Since $S$ is a closed surface (sphere), we use the Divergence Theorem:

$$\iint_{S} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS = \iiint_{V} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) \, dV$$

**Step 1: Calculate Divergence**

Given $\mathbf{F} = (2x + 3z)\hat{\mathbf{i}} - (xz+y)\hat{\mathbf{j}} + (y^2 + 2z)\hat{\mathbf{k}}$:

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x+3z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-xz-y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(y^2+2z)$$

$$= 2 + (-1) + 2$$

$$= 3$$

**Step 2: Transform Integral**

Substitute the constant divergence back into the volume integral:

$$\text{Integral} = \iiint_{V} 3 \, dV = 3 \times (\text{Volume of Sphere})$$

**Step 3: Calculate Volume**

The sphere has radius $r=3$.

$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (3)^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (27) = 36\pi$$

**Step 4: Final Calculation**

$$\text{Integral} = 3 \times 36\pi = 108\pi$$

## 5__  
Evaluate double integral $\int \int_{s}^{} fn \, \mathrm{d}s$ where $F = x\hat{\mathbf{i}} + y\hat{\mathbf{j}} + (z^2-1)\hat{\mathbf{k}}$ and $s$ is closed surface by plane z = 0 and z = 1 and cylinder $x^2+y^2 = 4$, also verify divergence theorem<br><br>
**Theorem Statement**

$$\iiint_{V} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}) \, dV = \iint_{S} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS$$

Given $\mathbf{F} = x\hat{\mathbf{i}} + y\hat{\mathbf{j}} + (z^2-1)\hat{\mathbf{k}}$ on the region defined by $x^2+y^2 \le 4$ and $0 \le z \le 1$.

**1. Volume Integral (RHS)**

**Calculate Divergence:**

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial (z^2-1)}{\partial z} = 1 + 1 + 2z = 2 + 2z$$

**Evaluate Integral:** Using cylindrical coordinates ($dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$): 
Limits: $z \in [0, 1]$, $r \in [0, 2]$, $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$.

$$\text{RHS} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{1} (2 + 2z) r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$

Integrate w.r.t $z$:

$$\left[ 2z + z^2 \right]_0^1 = 3$$

Integrate w.r.t $r$:

$$\int_{0}^{2} 3r \, dr = \left[ \frac{3r^2}{2} \right]_0^2 = 6$$

Integrate w.r.t $\theta$:

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi} 6 \, d\theta = 12\pi$$

**2. Surface Integral (LHS)**

The surface $S$ consists of three parts.

**Surface 1: Top (**$z=1$**)**

- $\mathbf{n} = \hat{\mathbf{k}}$
    
- $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = z^2 - 1$. At $z=1$, $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = 1^2 - 1 = 0$.
    
- Flux = $0$.
    

**Surface 2: Bottom (**$z=0$**)**

- $\mathbf{n} = -\hat{\mathbf{k}}$
    
- $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = -(z^2 - 1)$. At $z=0$, $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = -(-1) = 1$.
    
- Flux = $\text{Area} \times 1 = \pi(2)^2 = 4\pi$.
    

**Surface 3: Lateral Side (**$x^2+y^2=4$**)**

- $\mathbf{n} = \frac{x}{2}\hat{\mathbf{i}} + \frac{y}{2}\hat{\mathbf{j}}$ (Radial normal)
    
- $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = x(\frac{x}{2}) + y(\frac{y}{2}) = \frac{x^2+y^2}{2}$.
    
- On the surface, $x^2+y^2=4$, so $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$.
    
- Flux = $2 \times (\text{Lateral Area}) = 2 \times (2\pi r h) = 2 \times (2\pi(2)(1)) = 8\pi$.
    

**Total Surface Integral:**

$$\text{LHS} = 0 + 4\pi + 8\pi = 12\pi$$

**Result**

$$\text{LHS} = 12\pi, \quad \text{RHS} = 12\pi$$

Theorem Verified.

## 6__  
Verify Stock theorem for the function, $f=x^2\hat{\mathbf{i}}+xy\hat{\mathbf{j}}$ integrated round the square in plane $z=0$ where sides are $x=y=0$ and $x=y=a$<br><br>
**Stokes' Theorem Formula**

$$\oint_{C} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_{S} (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} \, dS$$

Given $\mathbf{F} = x^2\hat{\mathbf{i}} + xy\hat{\mathbf{j}}$ on a square ($0 \le x, y \le a$).

**1. Line Integral (LHS)**

We evaluate $\oint (x^2 \, dx + xy \, dy)$ along the perimeter.

- **Horizontal Sides (**$dy=0$**):** The integral is $\int x^2 \, dx$.
    
    - Bottom ($y=0$): Goes $0 \to a$.
        
    - Top ($y=a$): Goes $a \to 0$.
        
    - These two cancel each other out. **Sum = 0**.
        
- **Vertical Sides (**$dx=0$**):** The integral is $\int xy \, dy$.
    
    - Left ($x=0$): Integral of $0 \cdot y \, dy$ is **0**.
        
    - Right ($x=a$): Integral of $a \cdot y \, dy$ from $0 \to a$.
        
        $$\int_{0}^{a} ay \, dy = a \left[ \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_0^a = \frac{a^3}{2}$$

**Total LHS:**

$$0 + \frac{a^3}{2} = \frac{a^3}{2}$$

**2. Surface Integral (RHS)**

**Calculate Curl:**

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \left( \frac{\partial (xy)}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial (x^2)}{\partial y} \right) \hat{\mathbf{k}} = (y - 0)\hat{\mathbf{k}} = y\hat{\mathbf{k}}$$

**Evaluate Integral:** Region is $0 \le x \le a$ and $0 \le y \le a$. Normal is $\hat{\mathbf{k}}$.

$$\text{RHS} = \int_{0}^{a} \int_{0}^{a} y \, dx \, dy$$

Integrate w.r.t $x$:

$$\int_{0}^{a} y [x]_0^a \, dy = \int_{0}^{a} ay \, dy$$

Integrate w.r.t $y$:

$$a \left[ \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_0^a = \frac{a^3}{2}$$

**Result**

$$\text{LHS} = \frac{a^3}{2}, \quad \text{RHS} = \frac{a^3}{2}$$

Theorem Verified.

## 7__  
Using Stock theorem, evaluate $\int_{c}(xy) \, \mathrm{d}x + (xy) \, \mathrm{d}y$ where ''$c$" is square in $xy$ plane with vertices (1, 0), (-1, 0), (0, 1) and (0, -1)<br><br>
**The Theorem**

Stokes' Theorem converts the line integral into a double integral over the region $S$:

$$\oint_{C} P \, dx + Q \, dy = \iint_{S} \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) \, dA$$

**Step 1: Find the Integrand**

Given the integral $\int (xy) \, dx + (xy) \, dy$:

- $P = xy$
    
- $Q = xy$
    

Calculate the partial derivatives:

- $\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xy) = y$
    
- $\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(xy) = x$
    

The term inside the double integral is:

$$\left( y - x \right)$$

**Step 2: Setup the Integral**

We need to evaluate:

$$I = \iint_{S} (y - x) \, dA$$

**Step 3: Solve using Symmetry (Simplest Method)**

The region $S$ is a square centered at the origin $(0,0)$ with corners at $(1,0), (-1,0), (0,1), (0,-1)$.

Because the shape is perfectly symmetric around the origin:

1. **Average value of** $y$**:** For every point with a positive $y$, there is a matching point with a negative $y$. The sum (integral) of $y$ over this region is **0**.
    
2. **Average value of** $x$**:** For every point with a positive $x$, there is a matching point with a negative $x$. The sum (integral) of $x$ over this region is **0**.
    

$$\iint_{S} y \, dA = 0$$

$$\iint_{S} x \, dA = 0$$

Therefore:

$$I = 0 - 0 = 0$$

**Final Answer**

$$0$$
